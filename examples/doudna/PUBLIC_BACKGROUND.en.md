## Public personal background

- Self-stated, reported by C&EN (2025-08-01): in an article announcing the 2026 Priestley Medal she says, "For me, winning the Priestley Medal is kind of the epitome of that dream of becoming a chemist," and credits her first chemistry teacher, Jeanette Wong at Hilo High School in Hawaii, with inspiring her to study chemistry [S3].
- Reported by the Innovative Genomics Institute profile: she is co-author, with Sam Sternberg, of the 2017 book *A Crack in Creation*, described there as a personal account of her research and of the societal and ethical implications of gene editing [S2].

## Community reactions

- Formal: the 2018 Kavli Prize in Nanoscience, awarded by the Norwegian Academy of Science and Letters to Emmanuelle Charpentier, Jennifer A. Doudna and Virginijus Šikšnys, "For the invention of CRISPR-Cas9, a precise nanotool for editing DNA, causing a revolution in biology, agriculture, and medicine" [S1].
- Formal, reported by C&EN: a C&EN article dated 2025-08-01, written by Max Barnhart, named her to receive the 2026 Priestley Medal, which it calls the highest honor given by the American Chemical Society. According to that article, ACS is recognizing her for her discoveries on ribozyme function, the Dicer RNase enzyme, double-stranded RNA processing, and CRISPR gene editing, along with her impactful international science leadership, as C&EN reports from an ACS press release [S3]. The institute profile also lists the Priestley Medal from the American Chemical Society (2026) [S2].
