# Public background, presence and reactions: Thomas Hunt Morgan

Prepared: 2026-11-27, China Standard Time. Purpose: a short public note on personal background, public reactions and public presence for the worked example in `examples/morgan/`. This note is not an assessment of the person. Public information only. See `profile/PRIVACY.md`. Every line ends with a source tag, or says not confirmed.

Morgan died in 1945. Family details that appear in published biographies are left out of this note on purpose.

## Public personal background

- Reported by the Nobel Foundation biographical sketch and facts page: born 25 September 1866 in Lexington, Kentucky; died 4 December 1945 in Pasadena, California [S1][S2].
- Reported by the same biographical sketch: he took a B.S. degree at the University of Kentucky in 1886 and a Ph.D. at Johns Hopkins University in 1890, where he studied morphology with W. K. Brooks and physiology with H. Newell Martin [S1].
- Reported by a Columbia University magazine essay by Eric R. Kandel: the Johns Hopkins doctorate was for work on the development of sea spiders [S5].
- Reported by the Nobel biographical sketch: in 1887, the year after his graduation, he spent time at the seashore laboratory of Alphaeus Hyatt at Annisquam, Massachusetts; in the summer of 1888 he worked for the United States Fish Commission at Woods Hole; in 1890 he spent the summer at the Marine Biological Laboratory in Woods Hole, beginning a long association there as a summer investigator and trustee [S1].
- Reported by the Nobel biographical sketch: in 1890 he was awarded the Adam Bruce Fellowship and visited the marine laboratory at Naples, returning in 1895 and 1900 [S1].
- Reported by the Nobel biographical sketch: associate professor of biology at Bryn Mawr College from 1891 to 1904; professor of experimental zoology at Columbia University from 1904 to 1928 [S1].
- Reported by the Nobel biographical sketch: in 1928 he became professor of biology and director of the G. Kerckhoff Laboratories at the California Institute of Technology in Pasadena, remaining until 1945; in later years he had a private laboratory at Corona del Mar, California [S1].
- Reported by the Online Archive of California finding aid for his papers at Caltech: Caltech officials approached him in 1927 about setting up a new biology program, and in 1928 he began that last phase of his career [S6].
- Reported by Kandel: at Columbia, the Drosophila laboratory in Schermerhorn Hall, Room 613, became known as the Fly Room [S5].
- Reported by the Nobel biographical sketch: his early published work was critical of Mendelian conceptions of heredity; in 1909 he began the work on *Drosophila melanogaster* with which his name is associated [S1].

## Community reactions

- Formal: the 1933 Nobel Prize in Physiology or Medicine, awarded to him alone, "for his discoveries concerning the role played by the chromosome in heredity." His affiliation at the time of the award was the California Institute of Technology [S2].
- Formal: the Nobel biographical sketch records that he was made a Foreign Member of the Royal Society of London in 1919, delivered the Croonian Lecture in 1922, and received the Society's Darwin Medal in 1924 and Copley Medal in 1939 [S1].
- Named scientific address: on 20 January 1922, *Science* published William Bateson's address "Evolutionary Faith and Modern Doubts." After describing earlier doubts, Bateson wrote that those doubts "can not as regards the main thesis of the Drosophila workers, be any longer maintained," and that "The arguments of Morgan and his colleagues, and especially the demonstrations of Bridges, must allay all scepticism as to the direct association of particular chromosomes with particular features of the zygote" [S3][S4].

## Public social media and web presence

- There are no personal social media accounts. He died on 4 December 1945 [S2].
- Official Nobel Foundation pages: a biographical sketch, a facts page, and his Nobel Lecture of 4 June 1934, "The Relation of Genetics to Physiology and Medicine" [S1][S2][S7].
- Archival collection: the Online Archive of California publishes a finding aid, "Thomas Hunt Morgan Papers, 1916-1946," held by the Archives of the California Institute of Technology. The finding aid describes the papers as five linear feet in ten containers, and says they include correspondence, technical and course material, and papers relating to the formation of the Biology Division at Caltech [S6].
- National Academy of Sciences biographical memoir: a memoir of Morgan by A. H. Sturtevant was published in the Academy's *Biographical Memoirs* in 1959. Kandel cites it as volume 33, page 295. The Academy's current file address is https://www.nasonline.org/wp-content/uploads/2024/06/morgan-thomas-hunt.pdf. The memoir text has not been read: a request for that file was refused, so it is not confirmed [S5][S8].
- Royal Society memoir: Crossref records an obituary memoir, "Thomas Hunt Morgan, 1866-1945," by R. A. Fisher, in *Obituary Notices of Fellows of the Royal Society*, volume 5, issue 15, pages 451-466, issued 1947-02-28, doi:10.1098/rsbm.1947.0011. The memoir text has not been read, so its wording is not confirmed [S9].
