# Paragraph continuity diagnostic

This reference diagnoses order; it does not generate prose.

Check continuity only when:

- one experiment spans multiple paragraphs;
- a prediction is tested in the next paragraph;
- a paragraph leaves a question explicitly unanswered;
- a heading separates an action from its result.

Temporarily remove the heading or blank line and ask whether events still read in temporal and causal order. If not, reorder paragraphs or move the heading. Do not add a transition merely to make adjacent paragraphs appear connected.

Methods belong where readers need to know how a measurement remained valid. Once an experiment or question is complete, the paragraph may close and the next one may begin with a new time, person, material, or action.

Every sentence still needs a recoverable subject and a complete action, observation, or judgment. Read eight consecutive paragraphs for repetitive openings, repeated short endings, and unnatural transitions.
