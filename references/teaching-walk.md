# Teaching walk

English explainers supply only the order in which a non-specialist asks questions. They do not supply Chinese sentences.

1. Identify the reader's likely misunderstanding and state the narrow question.
2. Add only the prerequisite concepts needed for the current inference.
3. Introduce one analogy, symbol, or working hypothesis that produces an observable prediction; state its boundary.
4. Move through familiar phenomenon → mechanism → prediction → measured evidence → bounded conclusion.
5. Let figures carry spatial, temporal, or comparative relations that prose handles poorly.
6. End with a current paper or documented reception history, then leave a concrete next question.

For a historical experiment, ask in ordinary order: what was selected, what was crossed or compared, what appeared, what was counted, what alternative remained, and what later test distinguished it.

Continuity is diagnostic only. Check paragraph order when one experiment, prediction, or unanswered question spans multiple paragraphs. A completed passage may end normally; never add a compulsory transition sentence.

Depth comes from evidence layers and explicit boundaries, not terminology density, chronology dumps, or career padding.
