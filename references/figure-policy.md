# Figure policy

## Minimum

A biography normally needs:

1. a verified portrait of the person;
2. a legally reusable primary-source table/plate or numbered journal figure;
3. explanatory images only where they reduce the cost of understanding a relation.

For copyrighted journal figures, linking readers to the paper may be safer than redistribution. A citation is not permission to copy an image.

## Historical and explanatory assets

Prefer Wikimedia Commons public-domain or openly licensed files. Verify identity, creator, source page, and license; a filename is not proof. Record every local asset in `figures/SOURCES.md`.

Caption order: person/object → date or context → creator/source → license → citation. Add a negative clarification only for a realistic risk that readers will mistake the image for the actual experiment, specimen, historical scene, or paper figure.

## Paper evidence

Use the figure, table, plate, or in-text diagram that carries evidence. Do not use a title page, full page of running text, bibliography screenshot, or graphical abstract as the literature image.

## Generated mechanism diagrams

Use an illustration companion only for an otherwise invisible mechanism or evidence chain. Do not generate historical faces or fake documentary scenes. Save the exact generation instructions beside the asset and label the caption `机制示意，非历史照片`.

## Paths

Store assets under `figures/`. Reader-facing image destinations must not contain internal IDs. Every Markdown image path must resolve from the file containing it.
