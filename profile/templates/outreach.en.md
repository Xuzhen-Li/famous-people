# First email and conversation questions (English)

Use after the profile is done. Follow the contact instruction in section 12 of the profile first. If the lab page says how to apply, do that and do not email around it.

## First email: skeleton

Keep it under 150 words. Send it from your own institutional or personal address. Never send a message that implies a source you did not read.

Subject: <Prospective PhD student / postdoc / collaborator>: <one phrase on the shared topic>

Dear Professor <family name>,

I am <name>, <current position and institution>. I read <paper, year, DOI> and <second paper>. I was interested in <one specific point, in your own words>, because <how it connects to your own work, in one sentence>.

I am looking for <a PhD place starting in term year / a postdoc post / a collaboration on topic>. My background: <two lines: skills and one result with a link>. I saw on <lab page or admissions page, date> that <what it says about openings or how to apply>; I am writing to follow that instruction. <If it applies: I have attached my CV and one page on a project idea.>

Would you be able to tell me <one short question that the web pages do not answer>?

Thank you for your time.
<name, institution, link to your own page>

Things to leave out: praise that could be about anyone, a list of everything you have done, any claim you cannot back, anything about the researcher’s life outside work.

## Questions for the researcher

1. Which project would a new student or postdoc most likely start on, and who would supervise it day to day?
2. How are authorship and credit decided, and can I see an example from a recent paper?
3. How often does the group meet, and what does a typical week look like?
4. Where have the last three or four trainees gone, and how did the group help them get there?
5. How long is the funding for the post I would hold, and what happens if a grant ends?
6. How do you handle a project that stops working after a year?

## Questions for current or former members

Ask politely, one or two at a time, and accept a no. Do not ask anyone to say something negative about their supervisor. Do not quote anyone without their permission.

1. What did a normal first six months look like for you?
2. How easy is it to reach the researcher when you need a decision?
3. What kind of person does well in this group, and what kind finds it hard?
4. Did you feel free to work on your own idea?
5. What do you wish you had known before you joined?

## After the replies

Add what you learned to section 8 of the profile as Signal items with the date and who said it, only if the person agrees you may record it. Keep what you were told in private out of any shared copy.
